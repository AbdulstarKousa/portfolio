Computer vision · 2021

Teaching neural networks to colour grayscale images.

Exploring how convolutional networks and conditional generative models can infer plausible colours from black-and-white photographs.

Input

Grayscale images

Architectures

CNN · Attention U-Net · GAN

Evaluation

PSNR · SSIM · LPIPS

A grayscale image has no single correct colour.

Turning black-and-white pictures into plausible colour images is an underdetermined task: a single grayscale value may correspond to different real-world colours.

That makes architecture selection and evaluation interesting. Visual plausibility is not always captured by a single numerical metric.

Compare different ways to predict colour.

The study explored image-to-image modelling using convolutional architectures, attention-based U-Net components and adversarial training.

Work in a perceptual colour space

Explore L*a*b* colour space and predict colour information from the luminance channel.

Experiment with model families

Compare CNN-based and conditional generator–discriminator approaches, including attention U-Net and GAN variants.

Use complementary metrics

Examine PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS to compare reconstruction quality and perceptual similarity.

Lessons from image generation.

Colourization is inherently ambiguous

A predicted colour image may look plausible even when it differs from the ground-truth image.

Architecture choices involve trade-offs

Convolutional, attention-based and adversarial approaches have different strengths and training considerations.

Metric choice changes the story

PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS provide distinct perspectives rather than a universally sufficient quality measure.

Evaluation should match the real task.

Not every machine learning problem has a single objectively correct prediction. Colourization is a useful example of why evaluation needs both technical measures and thoughtful interpretation.

The project developed experience working with generative models and comparing their outputs systematically.
