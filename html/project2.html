Projects/Detecting changes in everyday app usage.

Time-series analysis · 2022

Detecting changes in everyday app usage.

Understanding how smartphone habits evolve by preparing, analysing and forecasting daily behavioural signals across a large user sample.

Dataset

Nearly 10,000 smartphone users

Focus

Daily app usage and behaviour change

Methods

Prophet · SARIMA · PELT

A changing habit is difficult to forecast.

Daily screen time, phone-check frequency and app usage can fluctuate substantially. Missing observations, seasonality and shifts in behaviour complicate the interpretation of a single time series.

The goal was to explore how statistical analysis and forecasting can help distinguish recurring patterns from meaningful changes in usage.

From noisy activity to interpretable trends.

The project used exploratory time-series analysis, data preparation and forecasting benchmarks to understand behaviour over time.

Make the signals usable

Detect outliers, fill missing observations, examine rolling windows, and explore transformations and stationarity.

Compare forecasting approaches

Evaluate a persistence baseline, seasonal ARIMA and Prophet on daily numbers of phone checks.

Investigate shifts

Use change-point analysis, including PELT, to explore potential changes in smartphone behaviour.

What the experiments indicated.

Prophet performed best in the reported comparison

The original project reports that Prophet outperformed persistence and seasonal ARIMA for forecasting 30 days of phone-check counts across an experiment involving 300 users.

Context matters for time-series analysis

Exploring seasonality, autocorrelation and missing data helped build a better understanding of the behavioural signals before modelling.

Change detection extends the analysis

Change-point methods offered a complementary perspective to forecasting by looking for shifts in usage patterns.

The simplest baseline deserves a seat at the table.

Forecasting is a comparison problem, not just a modelling problem. Testing a method against plausible alternatives matters more than choosing the most fashionable algorithm.

The project also reinforced the value of careful preprocessing and communicating what can, and cannot, be inferred from behavioural data.
